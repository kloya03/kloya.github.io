Learning to control a fish-like robot

From a physics-based simulator to predictive control on a swimming robot.

Research footage from the fish-like robot project. Playback-speed labels appear in the footage where applicable.

The challenge

An internally actuated swimmer has no direct thrust input. Forward motion and turning emerge from coupled body, rotor, tail, and fluid dynamics, making accurate prediction central to control.

Modeling, learning, and experiments

I designed, built, and tested the fish-like robot and developed a physics-based simulator. The learning framework uses simulation to initialize a Koopman predictor and experimental trajectories to improve its representation of the physical robot.

From prediction to control

The predictor supports model predictive control for path tracking. This project brings together nonlinear mechanics, system identification, sensing, and closed-loop experiments. The associated manuscript is submitted to IEEE Transactions on Robotics.
