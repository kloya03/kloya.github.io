Differentiable learning for swimming control

Learning a path-tracking controller through the dynamics of a fish-like robot.

Research footage from the fish-like robot project. Playback-speed labels appear in the footage where applicable.

The challenge

Swimming speed and turning are coupled. A controller must coordinate the effects of internal actuation and tail steering while following a desired path.

Learning through dynamics

Our collaborative work uses a reduced-order model with articulated tail links for offline training. Backpropagation through time learns adaptive PID gains, and pure-pursuit guidance supplies a reference heading.

On the physical robot

The learned controller is deployed for velocity and heading tracking on the swimming platform. This project connects modeling and learning with real robot demonstrations and is associated with our IROS 2026 paper.
